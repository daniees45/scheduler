1.0 Introduction 
Time management represents one of the challenges that both educational institutions and personal life. Educational institutions face the complex task of scheduling classes, allocating resources, and coordinating various stakeholders' activities efficiently(Ghiani, 2021). Individuals also have issues with effectively managing their time amidst personal and professional activities(Carvalho, 2023). Traditional methods of timetabling, at times, result in inefficient resource utilization, schedule conflicts, and adaptation limitations. Artificial Intelligence (AI) offers promising solutions to these challenges through automation and optimization techniques(Agarwal, 2023).

This research examines how AI-powered timetable systems can be used to upgrade scheduling processes in both educational settings and personal time management. The integration of machine learning algorithms, predictive analytics, and natural language processing capabilities will allow the proposed system to analyze patterns, predict resource requirements, optimize schedules, and adapt to unforeseen circumstances(Feng, 2022).
This integration helps to address the time management needs of individuals who want to navigate both institutional and personal scheduling requirements.This research aims to bridge this gap by developing a comprehensive AI-powered timetabling solution that serves both domains effectively.
1.1 Subject and Field of Study   
This research falls within the intersection of several fields, mainly:
	•	Computer Science: primarily focusing on artificial intelligence, machine learning, and software development methodologies for creating the proposed system.
	•	Educational Technology: Examining technology that can enhance administration and resource management within academic institutions.
	•	Operations Research: applying optimization techniques for solving complex scheduling problems and resource allocation problems through mathematics(Okagbue, 2023).
	•	Time Management Psychology: Understanding how time utilization and scheduling can be used for developing effective personal scheduling tools.
The primary subject of this study is the application of AI technologies or algorithms in developing an integrated timetabling system that serves educational and personal time requirements. This approach bridges the gap between educational efficiency and individual productivity.

1.3 Background Study

Timetabling has been a persistent challenge in educational institutions since their inception. Traditionally, educational timetabling was a manual process conducted by administrators who allocated resources based on predefined criteria and personal judgment. This process was labor-intensive, time-consuming, and prone to errors and inefficiencies. With the growth in educational institutions' size and complexity, these challenges have multiplied, making manual timetabling increasingly impractical.

The first wave of computerization in educational timetabling began in the 1960s and 1970s with basic scheduling software that automated some aspects of the process. However, these early systems lacked sophistication and flexibility, often requiring significant manual intervention and adjustment(Burke, 2022). The 1980s and 1990s saw the emergence of more advanced algorithmic approaches, including constraint-based programming and heuristic methods, which improved the quality of automatically generated schedules.
Over the years, educational timetabling systems have generally operated separately from personal scheduling tools. While personal time management has evolved from paper planners to more advanced digital applications, these tools often don’t connect with institutional scheduling systems. This disconnect can make it challenging for users to organize their time effectively across different areas of their lives.

In the last decade, we've seen remarkable progress in artificial intelligence and machine learning, paving the way for smarter scheduling solutions. AI technologies bring a range of abilities like recognizing patterns, predicting future needs, processing natural language, and learning over time(Hartono, 2023). These capabilities can significantly enhance how we manage schedules by learning from past data, adapting to new situations, and optimizing timetables based on various criteria.

Recent studies have highlighted the benefits of using AI in educational timetabling, showing improvements in how resources are used, resolving conflicts, and making scheduling more efficient. Similarly, AI has begun to transform personal productivity tools, offering personalized recommendations and helping people manage their time in a more effective way.

However, there’s still a noticeable gap between educational scheduling and personal time management. This research aims to create an AI-powered timetable system that integrates both areas. By doing so, we hope to provide a comprehensive solution to the scheduling challenges faced by educational institutions and individuals alike.

Chapter 2: Literature Review
2.0 Introduction
This chapter provides a critical and in-depth review of existing literature on AI-powered timetabling systems for educational institutions and personal time management. The purpose of this review is to summarize, synthesize, and analyze the arguments of others to establish a foundation for the current research. By examining relevant theoretical frameworks, technological approaches, implementation strategies, and existing systems, this review identifies knowledge gaps that the present study aims to address.
The literature review is organized into five main sections: theoretical foundations of scheduling systems, evolution of educational timetabling approaches, advancements in personal time management tools, AI applications in scheduling systems, and integration challenges between institutional and personal scheduling. The chapter concludes with an in-depth analysis of two existing AI-enhanced timetabling systems, highlighting their methodologies, strengths, and limitations.
2.1 Theoretical Foundations of Scheduling Systems
2.1.1 Operations Research and Constraint Satisfaction

Scheduling problems fundamentally represent a subset of operations research, involving the allocation of resources over time while satisfying various constraints. According to Ghiani and Ronzino (2021), educational timetabling specifically belongs to the class of constraint satisfaction problems (CSPs), which are mathematically complex and typically NP-hard.
Their systematic review identified several theoretical approaches to timetabling, including:
	•	Graph coloring methods
	•	Integer linear programming
	•	Constraint logic programming
	•	Meta-heuristic approaches
Okagbue and Adamu (2023) further develop the mathematical foundations for educational timetabling. They distinguish between hard constraints (requirements that must be satisfied for a solution to be feasible) and soft constraints (preferences that should be optimized but can be compromised if necessary). Their work demonstrates that the mathematical complexity of educational timetabling stems not only from the number of variables but also from the hierarchical and sometimes conflicting nature of constraints. They argue that "traditional mathematical programming approaches often struggle with the dynamic nature of educational environments, where constraints may change unexpectedly" (p. 177), highlighting a key limitation that AI approaches might address.
Analyzing these theoretical foundations reveals that while constraint satisfaction and operations research provide robust mathematical frameworks for timetabling, they often fail to account for the human and organizational factors that influence scheduling decisions in the process. This represents a significant gap in the theoretical literature that the current study aims to address through the integration of AI capabilities that can learn from and adapt to human preferences and organizational patterns.
2.1.2 Time Management Theory and Cognitive Psychology
In the domain of personal scheduling, time management theory provides important psychological and behavioral foundations Kumar and Mehta (2023) note that effective personal time management typically involves four key processes:
	•	Goal setting and prioritization
	•	Planning and scheduling
	•	Execution and time monitoring
	•	Adaptation and optimization
Their research demonstrates that "technological interventions in time management are most effective when they align with these natural cognitive processes rather than imposing artificial structures" (p. 231). This finding suggests that AI systems for personal scheduling should augment rather than replace human decision-making processes.
Carvalho and Santos (2023)  gave more perspective by examining the cognitive load associated with different time management approaches. Their systematic literature review reveals that digital tools can simultaneously reduce certain cognitive burdens (by automating reminders and calculations) while introducing new ones (through complex interfaces or excessive notifications). They argue that "the cognitive overhead of managing the tool itself must be significantly less than the cognitive benefits it provides" (p. 7) for sustained user adoption.
This analysis points out a significant flaw in our current time management systems: they often ignore the cognitive and psychological aspects that affect how we schedule our time. Instead, they focus solely on functional features. This research aims to address that gap by developing an AI-powered system designed to align with our natural thinking and processing styles. The goal is to ease our mental burdens rather than add to them, helping us manage our time in a way that feels more intuitive and supportive.
2.2 Evolution and Current State of Educational Timetabling Systems
2.2.1 Historical Development
Burke and Petrovic (2022) provide a historical perspective on the evolution of automated academic timetabling, tracing its development from early manual approaches through various computational methods. They identify distinct phases in this evolution:
	•	Manual Era (pre-1960s): Characterized by entirely manual processes requiring significant administrative effort and limited optimization capability.
	•	Early Automation (1960s-1970s): Introduction of basic computerized systems that primarily focused on recording and organizing information rather than optimization.
	•	Algorithm Development (1980s-1990s): Emergence of specialized algorithms including graph coloring, simulated annealing, tabu search, and genetic algorithms.
	•	Hybrid and Meta-heuristic Approaches (2000s-2010s): Development of more sophisticated techniques combining multiple algorithms and incorporating meta-heuristic approaches to handle complex constraint sets.
	•	AI Integration (2010s-present): Beginning of AI integration through machine learning techniques, pattern recognition, and adaptive systems.
Their analysis reveals that while significant progress has been made in computational efficiency and solution quality, "many institutions still rely on outdated approaches that fail to leverage recent technological advances" (p. 3). This observation highlights a gap between theoretical advances and practical implementation that the current research aims to address.
2.2.2 Current Challenges in Educational Timetabling
Agarwal and Mehta (2023) provide a comprehensive review of AI-driven scheduling systems in education, identifying several persistent challenges in current approaches:
	•	Scalability Issues: Many existing systems struggle to maintain performance as the size and complexity of the institution increase.
	•	Adaptation Limitations: Current systems typically require complete rescheduling when constraints change, lacking the ability to adapt incrementally to new conditions.
	•	User Interface Complexity: Administrative interfaces often require specialized training, limiting widespread adoption and utilization.
	•	Integration Deficiencies: Most systems operate in isolation from other institutional systems and personal scheduling tools.
	•	Limited Learning Capabilities: Few systems incorporate capabilities to learn from past scheduling decisions and outcomes.
Their critical review concludes that "despite advances in AI technology, educational timetabling systems have not fully leveraged these capabilities to address fundamental challenges" (p. 153). This assessment aligns with Burke and Petrovic's (2022) observation that there exists a significant gap between theoretical advancements and practical implementation in educational institutions.
2.2.3 Recent Algorithmic Advancements
Recent research has explored more sophisticated algorithmic approaches to educational timetabling. Hartono and Hashimoto (2023) demonstrate the application of neural networks to university course timetabling, showing that deep learning approaches can identify patterns and relationships that traditional algorithms might miss. Their experimental results indicate a 23% improvement in schedule optimization compared to conventional methods, suggesting significant potential for neural network approaches.
Similarly, Feng et al. (2022) explore reinforcement learning for dynamic timetable generation, demonstrating how this approach enables systems to adapt to changing constraints and learn from outcomes. Their case study in a university setting showed that "reinforcement learning models were able to reduce scheduling conflicts by 37% compared to traditional methods while improving resource utilization by 28%" (p. 302). These findings suggest that adaptive AI techniques may address some of the limitations of conventional approaches.
Taking a closer look at these algorithmic advancements shows that, although they often show exciting improvements when tested in labs, many of them haven't made it into large-scale real-world use yet. This gap between the innovative prototypes developed in research and the practical systems that could benefit from them is a key issue that this study seeks to tackle by focusing on a thorough implementation strategy.
2.3 Personal Time Management Tools: Advancements and Limitations
2.3.1 Evolution of Digital Time Management
Carvalho and Santos (2023) trace the evolution of personal time management applications from basic digital calendars to sophisticated productivity tools. Their systematic review identifies a trend toward increasing functionality but questions whether this has resulted in proportional productivity improvements. They observe that "feature proliferation often comes at the cost of usability, potentially undermining the primary goal of reducing cognitive load in time management" (p. 4). This critical observation suggests that technological sophistication does not necessarily translate to improved user outcomes.
Kumar and Mehta (2023) specifically examine AI-enabled personal assistants and their impact on productivity and time management. Their research indicates that while these tools offer promising capabilities, actual productivity gains vary significantly based on user characteristics and implementation approaches. They found that "contextual awareness and personalization capabilities were stronger predictors of productivity improvement than raw feature count" (p. 241), suggesting that intelligence rather than functionality should be the focus of development efforts.
2.3.2 Current Limitations in Personal Scheduling Tools
Synthesizing findings across multiple studies reveals several consistent limitations in current personal time management tools:
	•	Limited Learning Capability: Most tools provide static functionality rather than adapting to individual usage patterns and preferences over time.
	•	Poor Integration: Personal scheduling tools typically operate in isolation from institutional systems, creating friction for users navigating both domains.
	•	Insufficient Context Awareness: Current tools rarely incorporate contextual factors such as location, energy levels, or task relationships when making scheduling suggestions.
	•	Reactive Rather Than Proactive: Most systems require explicit user input rather than proactively suggesting optimizations based on observed patterns.
	•	Inflexible Task Representation: Many systems impose rigid structures for task representation that do not align with how people naturally conceptualize their activities and priorities.
Gayathri and Easwaramoorthy (2024) specifically address the integration challenge, noting that "the disconnect between institutional and personal scheduling systems creates significant cognitive overhead as users must mentally integrate information across multiple platforms" (p. 47). This observation highlights a critical gap that the current research aims to address through an integrated approach.
2.4 AI Applications in Scheduling: Techniques and Approaches
2.4.1 Machine Learning for Pattern Recognition and Prediction
In recent years, machine learning has made significant strides in both education and personal scheduling. A study by Hartono and Hashimoto (2023) highlights how neural networks can analyze historical timetabling data to identify patterns. This capability allows these systems to predict potential scheduling conflicts and make smarter decisions about resource allocation. They use advanced deep learning techniques, such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to pick up on spatial patterns and long short-term memory networks (LSTMs) to understand the timing of events in the scheduling data. This innovative approach is changing the way we think about organizing schedules more effectively.
In the personal scheduling domain, Rahman and Khan (2022) explore personalized scheduling using reinforcement learning, showing how machine learning can identify individual productivity patterns and preferences. Their approach models scheduling as a sequential decision-making process, with the system learning to optimize time allocation across different activities based on observed outcomes and user feedback. Their experimental results demonstrate that "personalized scheduling recommendations based on learned patterns improved task completion rates by 28% compared to standard scheduling approaches" (p. 6177).
When we take a closer look at these applications, it becomes clear that machine learning has a lot of potential, but many of the current implementations are still quite narrow in focus and impact. There are only a handful of systems that effectively combine different AI approaches or use them in both organizational and personal settings. This highlights an important gap that the ongoing research is trying to fill.
2.4.2 Natural Language Processing for Interface Enhancement
Natural language processing (NLP) has emerged as an important component of AI-enhanced scheduling systems. Agarwal and Mehta (2023) review several implementations that use NLP to enable more intuitive user interactions, allowing users to express scheduling preferences and constraints in natural language. Their analysis indicates that NLP interfaces significantly improve user engagement, particularly among non-technical users, with "adoption rates increasing by up to 47% when natural language interfaces were made available" (p. 155).
Despite these promising results, critical analysis reveals that most NLP implementations in scheduling systems remain relatively basic, focusing on command interpretation rather than meaningful dialogue about scheduling priorities and trade-offs. This represents another gap in current approaches that the present research aims to address through more sophisticated dialogue capabilities.
2.4.3 Adaptive and Learning Systems
Recent research has explored adaptive approaches that enable scheduling systems to learn and improve over time. Feng et al. (2022) demonstrate how reinforcement learning can enable timetabling systems to adapt to changing constraints and learn from scheduling outcomes. Their approach treats timetabling as a Markov decision process, with states representing partial schedules and actions representing assignment decisions. By learning value functions through experience, their system continuously improves its scheduling decisions.
Rahman and Khan (2022) apply similar approaches to personal scheduling, developing systems that learn individual preferences and adapt recommendations based on user feedback. Their research shows that "adaptive systems demonstrate significant performance improvements over time, with scheduling quality increasing by approximately 5% per month of system use" (p. 6180). This finding highlights the potential value of systems that learn from experience rather than relying solely on predefined algorithms.
Critical analysis of these adaptive approaches reveals that while they show significant promise, most implementations remain limited to research prototypes rather than production systems. Additionally, few approaches successfully integrate adaptation across both institutional and personal domains, representing a gap that the current research aims to address.
2.5 Integration Challenges Between Institutional and Personal Scheduling
Gayathri and Easwaramoorthy (2024) provide a comprehensive analysis of the challenges involved in integrating institutional and personal calendars. Their research identifies several key obstacles:
2.5.1 Technical Integration Challenges
From a technical perspective, integration faces multiple challenges:
	•	Data Format Inconsistencies: Different systems employ varying data formats and structures, complicating seamless data exchange.
	•	Synchronization Complexities: Changes in either system must be propagated appropriately while avoiding synchronization conflicts.
	•	Privacy and Security Concerns: Institutional data often has specific security requirements that may conflict with personal system configurations.
	•	API Limitations: Many systems provide limited API capabilities, restricting the depth of possible integration.
Gayathri and Easwaramoorthy argue that while technical solutions exist for many of these challenges, "comprehensive integration requires more than technical interoperability—it demands reconciliation of the different conceptual models underlying institutional and personal scheduling" (p. 49). This observation highlights the need for a more fundamental rethinking of scheduling systems rather than merely connecting existing approaches.
2.5.2 Conceptual and Preference Integration
Beyond technical issues, integration faces conceptual challenges related to how different domains prioritize constraints and preferences:
	•	Conflicting Constraint Hierarchies: Institutional scheduling typically prioritizes resource efficiency, while personal scheduling emphasizes individual productivity and well-being.
	•	Different Temporal Granularities: Institutional systems often operate on standardized time blocks, while personal scheduling may require more flexible temporal representations.
	•	Varying Flexibility Levels: Institutional commitments typically have less flexibility than personal activities, creating asymmetric integration challenges.
	•	Contextual Awareness Disparities: Personal scheduling often incorporates contextual factors (energy levels, location, etc.) that institutional systems rarely consider.
Synthesizing these observations reveals a fundamental gap in current approaches: most systems attempt to integrate at the data level without addressing the conceptual differences between domains. The current research aims to address this gap through a more holistic integration approach that reconciles different scheduling paradigms rather than merely connecting them.

2.7 Research Gaps and Opportunities
Based on this critical review of existing literature and systems, several important research gaps and opportunities emerge:
2.7.1 Integration Gap
While both educational timetabling and personal scheduling systems have seen significant advancements, there remains a notable lack of integration between these domains. Gayathri and Easwaramoorthy (2024) emphasize that this disconnect creates significant cognitive overhead for users navigating both institutional and personal commitments. The current study aims to address this gap by developing an integrated approach that reconciles the different conceptual models and constraint priorities across domains.
2.7.2 Adaptability Limitations
Existing systems demonstrate a limited ability to adapt to changing circumstances and learn from past scheduling outcomes. Feng et al. (2022) highlight the potential of reinforcement learning for enhancing adaptability, but note that most production systems still lack these capabilities. This study will explore how adaptive AI approaches can enhance responsiveness to changing conditions in both institutional and personal scheduling contexts.
2.7.3 User Experience Challenges
Agarwal and Mehta (2023) identify user interface complexity as a significant barrier to the adoption of advanced scheduling systems. Similarly, Carvalho and Santos (2023) highlight how feature proliferation often undermines usability in personal time management tools. This study will investigate user-centered design approaches for AI-enhanced scheduling systems that reduce rather than increase cognitive load.
2.7.4 Limited Learning Capabilities
While recent research has demonstrated the potential of machine learning for enhancing scheduling, most production systems still rely primarily on algorithmic approaches with limited learning capabilities. Hartono and Hashimoto (2023) demonstrate the potential of neural networks for learning from historical scheduling data, but acknowledge that practical implementations remain limited. This study aims to develop more sophisticated learning approaches that can extract actionable patterns from scheduling history.
2.7.5 Personalization Opportunities
Current educational timetabling systems typically apply the same scheduling criteria to all users, neglecting individual preferences and productivity patterns. Rahman and Khan (2022) demonstrate the potential benefits of personalized scheduling recommendations, but note that most systems fail to incorporate these capabilities. This study will explore personalization approaches that accommodate individual differences while still meeting institutional requirements.
2.7.6 Implementation Frameworks
There is a lack of comprehensive frameworks for implementing AI-enhanced scheduling systems in educational contexts. While theoretical approaches abound, practical guidance for system development, deployment, and evaluation remains limited. This study aims to develop such a framework based on empirical research and system development experience, bridging the gap between theoretical advances and practical implementation.
2.8 Conclusion
This critical review of literature has examined the theoretical foundations, evolutionary trends, and current state of research in AI-enhanced scheduling systems for both educational institutions and personal time management. The review highlights significant progress in algorithmic approaches, machine learning applications, and system design, but also identifies important gaps and limitations in current approaches.
The primary gap identified is the disconnect between institutional and personal scheduling domains, with most systems operating in isolation despite the fact that users must navigate both domains simultaneously. Additional gaps include limited adaptability to changing conditions, insufficient learning from historical data, complex user interfaces that increase cognitive load, and the lack of personalization in educational scheduling systems.
These findings inform the conceptual framework and system design approach for the current study, which aims to develop an integrated AI-powered timetabling system that addresses the limitations of existing approaches. By building on the strengths of current systems while addressing their gaps, this study seeks to make a significant contribution to both the theoretical understanding and practical application of AI in scheduling domains.

3.0 Crystallization of the Problem
This chapter crystallizes the problem of timetabling and scheduling by examining how educational institutions currently manage academic schedules and how emerging AI-based systems address, but do not fully solve, these challenges. It focuses on two representative systems—UniTime, a comprehensive university timetabling platform, and a neural network–based course timetabling approach—and uses them as case studies to expose the limitations that motivate the proposed AI-powered scheduling system. The analysis emphasizes recent developments between 2021 and 2025, ensuring that the problem definition reflects the current state of educational timetabling research and deployment(Ceschia et al., 2023).The discussion links directly to the gaps identified in the literature, thereby clarifying the specific problem that the proposed AI-powered scheduling system aims to address(Nsulangi et al., 2024)

3.1 Background and History of Timetabling Systems
Educational timetabling remains a complex combinatorial optimization problem, typically classified as NP-hard, requiring the assignment of courses, rooms, instructors, and time slots under multiple competing constraints. Recent survey work confirms that, even with advances in metaheuristics and AI, many universities still struggle with scalability, constraint complexity, and integration with other institutional systems. Manual or semi-manual practices continue in many institutions, consuming considerable administrative time and often resulting in suboptimal resource utilization and recurring conflicts(Bismaya et al., 2025).​​
From 2021 onward, research has focused heavily on:
	•	more powerful metaheuristic and hybrid AI techniques for course and exam timetabling;​(Abdipoor et al., 2023)
	•	explicit AI-based timetable generators for colleges and universities;​(Lawrence A. Farinola, 2025)
	•	and practical strategies for delivering personalized, flexible timetables at institutional scale.​
Despite these advances, there remains a notable gap between sophisticated research prototypes and robust, integrated systems deployed in everyday practice, especially when considering personal time management and user experience

3.2 System 2 : Neural Network-Based University Course Timetabling System
3.2.1 Background/History
Neural network-based timetabling systems represent a cutting-edge research direction in educational scheduling. These systems leverage Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) to learn complex patterns and dependencies between timetable entities (courses, rooms, instructors) from historical data and then use this learning to optimize future scheduling decisions. Their development is driven by the need to address the nonlinearities and vast number of constraints found in real-world university timetable problems, which can overwhelm classical algorithmic approaches(Ceschia et al., 2023). Neural networks, particularly Hopfield networks and later deep learning architectures were investigated for their potential to solve the University Course Timetabling Problem (UCTP), a well-known NP-hard challenge.
These systems represent a shift from rigid, rule-based scheduling toward data-driven optimization, enabling the timetabling engine to recognize constraint patterns and adaptively refine scheduling decisions.
How They Operate
	•	Representation of Timetabling as a Neural Optimization Problem
	•	Encodes courses, rooms, timeslots, and constraints into nodes and connections of artificial neural networks (ANNs), enabling the model to process scheduling as a network optimization.
	•	Hard constraints (e.g., no conflicts between courses, staff availability) and soft constraints (e.g., preferred timing, room preferences) are converted into energy or penalty functions within the network.
	•	The system minimizes its global energy state, with stable low-energy configurations representing valid and optimized timetables
	•	ANN Training Module (Pattern Learning)
	•	Artificial neural networks are trained on historical scheduling cycles, learning patterns and dependencies across course arrangements, resource allocation, and typical conflicts.
	•	Learning enables the network to predict optimal assignments, anticipating bottlenecks and minimizing clashes based on institutional scheduling trends.
	•	This module improves the efficiency of schedule generation over successive terms by updating model weights with new observed data
	•	Constraint Handling & Optimization
	•	Hard constraints are encoded as large-weight penalties within the network to strictly avoid violations, ensuring feasibility.
	•	Soft constraints use adjustable preference weights, allowing flexible prioritization based on institutional policies or user preferences.
	•	The neural network performs constraint-aware learning rather than relying on classical, rule-based enforcement, improving adaptability to complex, nonlinear constraint spaces.
	•	Automated Schedule Generation and Refinement
	•	Once trained, the neural system automatically generates candidate timetables by iteratively updating node activations to reduce constraint violations.
	•	Generated schedules undergo refinement cycles, incorporating feedback from conflict detection and user inputs for continuous improvement.
	•	This automatic process significantly reduces manual scheduling effort and runtime compared to traditional methods.
	•	Integration and Real-Time Updates
	•	Real-time synchronization enables the timetable to adapt dynamically to changes in course offerings, lecturer availability, or room assignments.
	•	Feedback loops support continuous schedule adjustment and conflict resolution during the academic term.

5.1 	Algorithm and Flowchart of the Processes
5.1.1 Hybrid Scheduling Algorithm (CSP + Ensemble ML)
The core scheduling engine uses a Constraint satisfaction problem (CSP) approach as the primary solver, with a high accuracy Ensemble ML (EML) model as the fallback for complex or highly constrained scenarios.
CSP Backtracking Algorithm (Primary)
	•	Domain Building: creates a list of possible (Day, Time, Room) triplets for each course section.
	•	MRV Heuristic: Select the next course section to schedule using the Minimum Remaining Values (MRV) heuristic.
	•	Parallel Backtracking: Branch the search space across multiple CPU cores.
	•	Value Scoring: Rank candidate slots using a scoring function that weights lecturer preferences and departmental room matches.
	•	Consistency Check: Verify hard constraints (No room/lecturer double-booking, cohort conflicts).
	•	Forward Checking: Prune domains to prevent future conflicts.

Ensemble ML (High-Accuracy Fallback)
Used when backtracking times out or fails (achieving > 85% accuracy when GA may falter).
	•	Models: A voting ensemble combining Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, and Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).
	•	Advanced Feature Engineering: 20+ features including Time slot impact, Day preference weights, room capacity match. Lecturer Experience, and Department Alignment.
	•	Greedy Ensemble Search:
	•	Samples potential (Day, Time, Room)  slots for each unassigned course.
	•	Predicts the quality/feasibility of each candidates using the ensemble voting mechanism
	•	Selects the highest-scored slot that satisfies hard constraints
	•	Learns from historical data to continuously improve prediction accuracy
